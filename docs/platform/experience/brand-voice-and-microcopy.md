# BOBA Bear — Brand Voice, UX Writing & Microcopy Design System

**For The Unbothered.**

This is the **Founder-locked creative standard** for BOBA Bear brand identity, editorial naming, distinctive voice, and proposed UI writing. It is suitable for designers, writers, agencies, and AI agents to apply to new concepts and authorized brand/editorial content.

**Authority boundary:** This document is supporting brand guidance, **not** a new product-language, product-definition, acceptance, or architecture authority. **LANG-1 prevails** for customer promises, ordering terminology, functional state, accessibility, money, availability, Checkout, Payment, refunds, privacy, and all task-critical copy. The relevant approved Product/Experience contract governs behavior. This lock is **not** approval to change live site copy, navigation, routes, customer product semantics, or the current product slice.

## 1. Brand thesis

BOBA Bear is a lifestyle brand about appetite, individuality, self-expression, and creative connection. Food and drinks are its current commercial focus; Wear and Culture are prospective editorial and future brand expressions.

**Purpose before personality.** Make the brand memorable, the experience effortless, and every promise true.

**Unbothered** means self-assured, expressive, and comfortable being yourself. It never means careless service, indifference to an error, or dismissiveness toward a customer.

Voice pillars:

| Pillar | Write with | Never confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| Confident | A clear point of view | Arrogance or unsupported superiority |
| Playful | Wit and a light touch | Forced memes, jokes at a customer's expense |
| Expressive | Appetizing, original editorial storytelling | Generic hype or empty buzzwords |
| Welcoming | Inclusive, natural language | Gatekeeping or invented exclusivity |
| Responsible | Honest promises and clear actions | Fake scarcity, money or availability claims |

Our tone is consistent in character but adapts to the stakes: expressive for discovery; helpful for shopping; calm, literal, and precise around Checkout, Payment, errors, and support.

## 2. Locked creative names and brand architecture

| Internal meaning | Locked editorial identity | Purpose | Live capability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand world / home | **The Den** | Enter and explore BOBA Bear | Existing homepage remains at / |
| Wear | **The Fits** | Personal style and self-expression | Editorial design concept; no Wear store authorized |
| Culture | **The Scene** | Creative voices, stories, participation | Editorial design concept; no Culture store or event capability authorized |

Internal Food / Wear / Culture pillars are strategic categories, **not automatically navigation labels**. One connected BOBA Bear world takes precedence over three disconnected stores.

**The Den** is the editorial identity of the existing brand home. It is not a new route or an automatically promoted global navigation item. When a control needs it, use an explanatory accessible label such as **“The Den — explore BOBA Bear.”** On the homepage itself, the commercial action is **Order Now**, not “Explore The Den.”

**The Fits** and **The Scene** remain locked *creative names*, but are **not live retail destinations**. If shown before a real editorial destination is available, visibly identify them as **Design concept**, avoid deceptive hover/click behavior, and never show Shop Now, price, inventory, membership, events, or booking actions without an actual authorized capability.

**Drops** is a potential campaign/release format that can span pillars; it is not a fourth pillar or a second pricing/catalog authority. The current Drops navigation is the existing editorial jump to /#drops. A release may be called a Drop only when the underlying story/product/activation exists and timing, availability, and scarcity are truthful.

## 4. Navigation and customer task vocabulary: preserved

**Current primary navigation remains: Menu · Drops · Sign In or My BOBA · Cart.**

- BOBA Bear logo continues to link to the current homepage.
- Menu is the ordering destination. **Order Now** is a purchase-entry CTA; **Browse Menu** is an editorial food-discovery CTA, never a second global destination.
- Cart is the in-progress purchase. Do not relabel it Bag, Order, or Basket.
- Signed-out customers see Sign In. My BOBA is a signed-in **account disclosure**, not a new My BOBA route. My Orders, Profile, Saved Addresses, and Sign Out are account destinations/actions.
- No The Den / The Fits / The Scene top-level navigation is authorized by this standard.
- Existing URL paths do not need renaming to follow the editorial vocabulary.

Task-facing labels retained from the approved customer-language direction: **Menu; Order Now; Cart; My BOBA; My Orders; Customize; Sign In; Add to Cart; Apply code; Pay securely; Delivery; Pickup; Choose a pickup location; Order confirmed.**

A branded headline may surround a literal task label:
- Heading: **Make it yours.**
- Helper: Choose the options you want; the actual menu determines any extra charges.
- Action: **Customize** or **Add to Cart**, as appropriate to the existing flow.

Do not force a different synonym into every screen. One customer concept should keep one stable functional name.

## 5. Tone by consequence

| Moment | Writing behavior |
|---|---|
| Brand, The Den, editorial Wear/Culture | Bold and evocative, with clear descriptors |
| Food and drinks browsing | Sensory, concrete, short; accurate to the sellable product |
| Product configuration | Friendly and instructional; required vs optional and paid vs included are clear |
| Cart | Simple, comfortable, explicit totals and editing |
| Coupons and Checkout | Precise and neutral; never invent a saving |
| Payment and payment uncertainty | Calm, literal; never encourage another pay while unresolved |
| Orders, refunds, help | Respectful, specific, actionable |
| Privacy, consent, legal | Plain and factual |
| Workforce and administration | Task-focused and operationally precise |

**No humor in money, payment uncertainty, refunds, errors, privacy, or security.** Never make the customer decode a branded euphemism to resolve a problem.

## 6. Microcopy and status discipline

The lines below describe writing *intent*. **Actual live wording remains source- and LANG-1-owned** until a separately authorized copy pass. Do not replace current messages just because a sample below reads better.

| Intent | Copy principle | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Cart empty | Say the Cart is empty and offer a real supported next step | Do not change existing CTA automatically |
| Item unavailable | Identify the specific product when the server establishes it; give remove/edit choice | No invented stock or outlet state |
| Delivery-only coupon on Pickup | Explain the actual mode restriction | Only if authoritative eligibility proves it |
| Cart conflict | Explain that the Cart changed and how to recover | Do not discard preserved intent |
| Price changed | Explain genuine payable difference and request reconfirmation | Equal displayed payable is not a monetary price change |
| Payment still checking | Say verification continues and another payment must not be attempted yet | Preserve the current approved live message |
| Order confirmed | Confirm only after authoritative order evidence | No invented kitchen timeline or ETA |
| Operator promotion without redeemable coupons | Clearly explain issuance versus activation | Only if operations data confirms zero issued codes |

The current payment waiting message and empty-Cart flow should remain as implemented; proposals in this brand guide do not supersede them.

**“A little extra?” is RESERVED COPY** for the planned, separately governed Cart curated-add-on shelf (IMP-036K). It does not activate recommendations, add a UI module, or allow Product Detail recommendations.

No fake countdowns, implied popularity, bestseller labels, artificial urgency, unverified dietary/allergen facts, guaranteed times, or unbacked discounts.

## 7. Style, international readability, and accessibility

- **Headlines:** expressive when appropriate; upper case is a design treatment, not an accessibility requirement.
- **Buttons:** action-led and predictable. Keep platform conventions for capitalization; do not style business logic into copy.
- **Body:** clear, short sentences in natural English; minimize exclamation marks.
- **Slang:** avoid forcing temporary internet slang, especially in transactional flows.
- **Emoji:** optional in brand/editorial work; never essential to understand a state or action.
- **Measurements and INR:** output only validated values from their authoritative source. Don't invent volume, tax, price, savings, or timing.
- **Accessibility:** branded names get explanatory descriptors or accessible labels where first-time comprehension requires it; icons require action-specific names. Errors and status changes must be announced and remain understandable without visual art or animation.
- **Channels:** the same customer promise must hold across web, future native apps, SMS/email/receipts, and support. The available capabilities may differ; the truth must not.

## 8. AI-agent and designer implementation rules

When proposing, editing, or shipping UI writing:

1. Identify the surface and actual customer purpose.
2. Classify the text: Brand, Editorial, Shopping, Transactional, Recovery, Operational, or Legal.
3. Read **LANG-1** and applicable Product/Experience contracts before changing task-facing or status language.
4. Use the locked names and editorial lines for new **brand-design concepts**. Do not auto-migrate the existing site.
5. Do not introduce new nav, routes, checkout controls, memberships, Wear/Culture stores, Drops mechanisms, reward wallets, or recommendation shelves through text alone.
6. Keep operational product data outside reusable hardcoded platform copy.
7. Preserve the live functional vocabulary, exact states, and customer money authority.
8. For ordinary copy refactors within authorized scope, implement/test without another research project; route material behavior or policy decisions to their owning authority.
9. Use semantic identifiers, not sprint/IMP-coded new source names. Example future copy-key patterns: brand.home.headline, navigation.menu, cart.empty.title, payment.pending.description. Do not build a new content infrastructure solely for these examples.
10. Verify real UI labels, screen-reader names, links, empty/error states, narrow mobile layouts, and regression evidence when shipping.

## 9. Brand-copy acceptance checklist

A proposed string is acceptable when:

- **Purpose:** The customer's objective is obvious.
- **Brand:** It feels distinctive rather than artificially slang-heavy.
- **Comprehension:** A first-time visitor can predict the destination or action.
- **Truth:** The system/business supports every claim.
- **Consistency:** Functional concepts use established names.
- **Accessibility:** Meaning survives without imagery or color.
- **Longevity:** Wording can age with the brand.
- **Safety:** The string does not disguise money, eligibility, failure, privacy, or security.

For Direction A concept reviews, specifically test whether users can find **Menu**, understand **The Den** as brand home, and understand **The Fits/The Scene** as editorial concepts rather than operating stores. This is design comprehension feedback, not a new product gate.
